Why a mascot works here specifically

The design audit found the aesthetic reads as surveillance — crosshairs, reticles, "contacts" on a radar dish. The fix needs warmth without abandoning a genuinely distinctive identity.

Volt is the cheapest warmth you can buy. The bolt is already your logo. Making it alive adds a friendly presence without changing a single token.

The rules

What Volt does, and what it must never do

An always-present assistant is how you get Clippy. Volt is silent by default — it appears at six scripted moments and is invisible everywhere else. It is a presence, not a feature.

Volt does

  • Appear at exactly six moments, then leave
  • Speak in 12 words or fewer
  • Lower the cost of the brave thing
  • Make leaving gracefully feel normal
  • Celebrate showing up — never streaks or logins
  • Give empty states a voice
  • Sit inside the layout, never float over it

Volt never

  • Speaks during SOS, report, or block — hard rule, no exceptions
  • Becomes a chatbot you type at
  • Follows you around the app
  • Nags, guilts, or uses streaks
  • Says "I" — it is not pretending to be a person
  • Speaks twice in one screen
  • Appears more than 3 times a day
The one rule that keeps it from becoming a liability

A cute character in a safety product can trivialise it. So Volt is structurally barred from every safety surface — SOS, report, block, verification, the moderation path. Those screens stay sober. If Volt can appear there, someone will eventually ship a cartoon next to an emergency button.

The pattern: Volt names the fear, not the feature. It never sells, never counts, never congratulates you for using the app — only for doing the difficult real-world thing the app exists for.

Build cost

One React Native component, an SVG with four animation states, and a table of six trigger conditions. No AI, no backend, no model call. Volt is scripted — which is what makes it shippable in days and impossible to hallucinate a wrong thing at someone standing outside a café.

Add the LLM later, if ever. The version that helps write a request note is the only one with a real case for it, and even that works as three tap-to-insert openers first.
